OHIO • EVIDENCE-BASED EMPLOYMENT GUIDE

Find employers willing to consider your next chapter.

Separate real second-chance programs from job ads that welcome applicants with records, time-restricted screening rules, and case-by-case reviews. No job offer or eligibility is guaranteed.

Evidence is not an offer of employment. A published job may be filled, and conviction-related eligibility can depend on the offense, the job, other qualifications and how an employer calculates time. Confirm details with the employer before applying. A "fair chance law" statement alone does not prove willingness to hire. No criminal-history data is collected on this page.

Understand the categories

Employer second-chance programEmployer directly documents hiring people after incarceration. Specific openings must still be confirmed.
Posting welcomes recordsThe vacancy listing explicitly welcomes applicants with criminal records. No time rule was documented.
5-, 7- or 10-year restrictionA particular vacancy says no convictions of a specified type within that period. Older records are not automatically approved.
Case-by-case / complianceEmployer mentions individualized review or fair chance legal compliance; no hiring promise or time cutoff verified.

Why there is no "3-year approved" filter: We have not yet substantiated a three-year policy for a current Ohio opportunity. Never confuse a three-year driving-history requirement with a three-year felony waiting period. A lookback period may run from conviction, offense, sentencing or release; ask the employer which date controls.
